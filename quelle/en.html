    Section 3

    Another Device Works Fine · Satellite

    One device on the installation works, the other does not. On satellite, a conflict between the devices is possible.

    Alle Einstellungen → Allgemein → Sender → Sendereinstellung (Programmsuche und -einstellungen) → Autom. Einstellung → Satelliteneinstellungen → Unicable → Ein → Unicable-Einstellungen

    LG counts differently from the label

    • Labels and converters usually count UB 1–4 or 1–8
    • The device menu often counts from 0 — label UB 3 is then number 2 in the menu
    • Question to ask: does the list in the menu start at 0 or at 1?
    • Only the frequency is unambiguous — enter the one from the label and the number becomes secondary
    • Common values: 1210 · 1420 · 1680 · 2040 MHz — other converters use e.g. 1284 · 1400 · 1516 MHz
    • Where they differ, the label wins, not the default list

    How to recognise Unicable

    • Multi-family building with one outlet per flat
    • The second TV loses the picture as soon as the first one runs — typically in the evening at prime time, when every device is on
    • The distributor is marked Unicable, SCR, SatCR, EN 50494 or EN 50607/JESS
    • The numbers are allocated by the antenna installer

    Possible causes

    • Two devices on the same UB number — the one that is switched on blocks the other completely
    • Unicable not enabled in the menu — the device sends DiSEqC instead of SCR commands
    • Number correct, transport frequency entered wrongly
    • Number off by 1 — the menu counts from 0, the label from 1
    • Wrong standard — installation is EN 50607 (Unicable 2 / JESS), device set to Unicable 1
    • DiSEqC or the 22 kHz tone still active — this disables Unicable

    First

    1. Unplug the other satellite device — standby is not enough
    2. Measure again — if signal appears, the number conflict is confirmed
    3. Read the label on the converter or outlet: UB number, transport frequency, standard

    Then

    1. Enter a free UB number and its matching frequency
    2. DiSEqC off, 22 kHz tone off, motor type off — these four are mutually exclusive: when Unicable is switched on, the device turns DiSEqC, the 22 kHz tone and the motor type off by itself. If one of them stays active, the device sends the wrong commands.
    3. Switch both devices on, measure again
    4. No success: try the number one lower and one higher
    5. No success: swap Unicable 1 for Unicable 2

    Section 4

    Another Device Works Fine · Cable

    One device on the outlet works, the other does not. On cable, a conflict between the devices is ruled out — every device gets the same signal at the same time.

    Possible causes

    • A device conflict is ruled out — every device gets the same signal at the same time, nothing is allocated
    • Connecting cable or plug between outlet and device faulty
    • One distributor output weaker or faulty
    • Search method or filter set wrongly on the device
    • If only encrypted channels are missing: CI+ module or smartcard
    • Level at this outlet too low — longer run, more distributors

    First

    1. Device straight to the outlet — no distributor, no intermediate cable, other device unplugged
    2. If it works then: the cause is on the way there — distributor, intermediate cable or plug
    3. If it still does not work: the cause is the outlet itself or the device
    4. Check the connecting cable and plug, change the distributor output
    5. Cross-check: put a working device on the same line

    Then

    1. Check search method and filter on the device, start the search again
    2. If only encrypted channels are missing: check the CI+ module and smartcard
    3. Measure the level at both outlets and compare the difference with the expected attenuation

    Section 8

    Fundamentals

    Level is the loudness. MER is the clarity.

    Level says how much arrives — raw power, measured in dBµV or dBm. Noise and interference count towards it. A high level on its own proves nothing at all.

    MER says how clean it is — the ratio of wanted signal to error, measured in dB. Higher is better, because MER does not count interference but measures the distance from it. At 0 dB, signal and interference would be equally large.

    Someone can speak loudly and indistinctly: high level, poor MER. Or quietly and perfectly clearly: low level, good MER.

    What each measure actually does

    An amplifier raises wanted signal and noise alike; an attenuator lowers both. The ratio is unchanged either way — neither measure improves the MER.

    Amplifiers only help with a low level and a good MER. If the MER has collapsed, there is interference in the signal that has to be found and removed: plug, shielding, faulty cable, a source of interference in the building.

    The most common mix-up: 9 dB is a usable limit on satellite and hopeless on cable. 256QAM has constellation points much closer together and therefore reacts more sensitively to noise.

    Percentage readings are not comparable. They are scaled per manufacturer and cannot be converted into dBµV or dBm. With DVB-C, strength readings around 50 to 60 percent are normal even with flawless reception.

    What the values mean

    ValueMeaning
    Signal strengthHow much power is present at the tuner input. Derived from the AGC control voltage. Noise and interference count towards it. On its own it says nothing about whether reception is possible.
    Signal qualityHow cleanly the data stream can be decoded. Calculated from MER/SNR and error rate. The value that decides.
    SNR / C/N / MER (dB)Noise margin. A physical quantity, not scaled by the manufacturer. Higher is better.
    Level (dBm / dBµV)Absolute power. Too high means overload.
    Headroom (link margin)The gap between the measured MER and the minimum for the modulation. Cable example: 35 dB measured, 30 dB minimum → 5 dB headroom.

    Why cable and satellite have different limits

    It is not the transmission path that decides but the modulation — how much information is packed into each symbol.

     SatelliteCable
    MethodQPSK / 8PSK256QAM
    Bits per symbol2 – 38
    MER requiredfrom 6 dBfrom 30 dB
    Tolerance to interferencehighlow

    Satellite is the lorry, cable the sports car

    QPSK has four possible states, far apart from one another — the receiver may be badly wrong and still land correctly. With 256QAM, 256 states sit on the same area, and even slight scatter lands in the neighbouring field.

    Satellite has to be robust, because the signal arrives weak and noisy from 36,000 km away. Cable can pack in more, because the medium is shielded — and does so, because bandwidth there is scarce.

    Practical consequence: a MER of 14 dB is a good value on satellite and useless on cable. The same number, two entirely different statements.

    What an LNB is, and why 9750 / 10600

    What an LNB is, and why 9750 / 10600

    LNB stands for low noise block converter. It sits at the focal point of the dish and has a single job: it receives the satellite frequencies between 10.7 and 12.75 GHz and converts them down to 950 to 2150 MHz. Without that step, carrying them in coaxial cable would be impossible — frequencies that high would vanish after a few metres.

    The satellite band is about 2050 MHz wide, but the cable run carries only some 1200 MHz. It is therefore split into two halves, each with its own oscillator:

    • 9750 MHz for the lower band, 10.7 to 11.7 GHz
    • 10600 MHz for the upper band, 11.7 to 12.75 GHz

    The tuner switches between the two with a 22 kHz signal. The arithmetic is plain subtraction:

    11362 MHz − 9750=1612 MHz
    12188 MHz − 10600=1588 MHz

    Why a wrong value blocks everything: if the TV holds a different oscillator frequency, the arithmetic yields the wrong intermediate frequency. The tuner then sits beside the actual channel and finds nothing — while the LNB keeps delivering full power. That is exactly where the picture of high strength with constant zero quality comes from.

    Other types: a single LNB feeds one device, a twin two, a quad four. A quattro LNB belongs on a multiswitch and does not work straight into a TV. A Unicable LNB carries several users over one cable and has to be set as such in the menu.
